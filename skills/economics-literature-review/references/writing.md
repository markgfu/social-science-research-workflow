# Model-Lineage Synthesis and Writing

## Lineage unit

For each theory paper, compare:

```text
predecessor
-> inherited baseline assumptions
-> changed assumption
-> new decision variable or constraint
-> new economic mechanism
-> new equilibrium/proposition/welfare result
```

Do not infer the predecessor from chronology alone. Require an author statement, explicit model reuse, or a transparent `CODEX INTERPRETATION` with evidence.

## Synthesis gate

Build the synthesis only from `accepted + A` notes. Peripheral B/C records may appear in a search-needed list but cannot support substantive claims.

For every proposed gap, record both evidence supporting the gap and evidence or search results that could contradict it. Use `needs-search` until the relevant lineages, terminology variants, and forward/backward citations have been checked.

## Section architecture

Prefer 2-5 economic lineages or mechanism families rather than author-by-author summaries. Within each section:

1. define the common economic question and benchmark;
2. state the shared assumptions;
3. show the sequence of economically meaningful modifications;
4. compare mechanisms and results;
5. identify what is established, disputed, or still search-bounded;
6. connect to the candidate project extension without declaring novelty prematurely.

## Paragraph contract

Adapt `research-paper-writing` as follows:

- one principal analytical message per paragraph;
- topic sentence states the comparison or lineage claim;
- representative papers are evidence, not the paragraph's organizing principle;
- distinguish author claims from synthesis and inference;
- end with a consequence for model selection, search, or the next lineage;
- reverse-outline every section after drafting.

## Claim-evidence ledger

Before accepting prose, map each major claim:

| Claim ID | Draft claim | Claim class | Supporting citekeys | Page/section | Evidence level | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Statuses are `supported`, `needs-evidence`, `needs-verification`, `needs-search`, or `remove/weaken`.

## Final review

Review as a skeptical economics referee:

- Is the benchmark model correctly identified?
- Are changed assumptions economically meaningful and accurately attributed?
- Are propositions and comparative statics anchored to the paper?
- Does the synthesis hide contradictory results?
- Is the candidate contribution actually novel, or merely not yet found?
- Does every strong statement have evidence proportional to its strength?
